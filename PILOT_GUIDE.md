# AjoConnect pilot guide and evidence sheet

**Status:** Proposed field protocol. This document is not a record of a completed pilot. Do not insert example results into the IEEE application as findings.

## 1. Pilot question

Can a trader who does not regularly use digital tools for trade complete a useful trade information task independently after seeing a demonstration from an existing, trusted àjọ group member? A follow-up after 30 days tests whether she continues to use the skill without assistance.

## 2. Before a session

- Obtain permission from market and àjọ leadership, and informed, voluntary consent from each participant. Participation must not affect their membership or access to services.
- Choose a quiet enough place to hear the trader. Explain what the demo can and cannot do, including its fictional sample prices.
- Use the trader's own device if she chooses. Ask about shared phone access, data costs and preferred language. Do not request account credentials, transaction details or private group messages.
- Select one real question that matters to the trader. If discussing price, compare equivalent units and quality and verify prices through independent trusted contacts. Never present the demo's sample figures as actual quotes.

## 3. Guide script for a 10 to 15 minute session

1. **Listen:** “What trading decision would you like to make more confidently?” Record the task category without recording private details.
2. **Demonstrate:** Show one relevant question in AjoConnect. Explain that a suggested answer is guidance and must be checked against a trusted source.
3. **Hand over:** Let the trader operate the phone and repeat the task, with no prompting unless she requests help. Note whether she completed it and what assistance she needed.
4. **Safety check:** Ask what she would verify before acting, especially the source, date, product unit, quality and payment confirmation.
5. **Follow up:** With separate consent, arrange a 30-day check. Ask for a fresh demonstration or a specific example of independent use. A statement of intent alone is not continued use.

## 4. Minimal anonymous session record

Use one row per participant. Assign a random participant code kept separately from contact details. Never put names or phone numbers into the evidence sheet.

| Field | Allowed entry |
| --- | --- |
| Participant code | Random local code |
| Session date and market | Date and general market name |
| Consent for observation | Yes or no |
| Device access | Own phone, shared phone, guide phone, other |
| Preferred language | Trader's own description |
| Task category | Price, payment check, supplier, group sharing, other |
| Guide demonstration completed | Yes or no |
| Independent task completed | Yes, partly, no, or not observed |
| Assistance needed | None, hint, hands-on assistance, or task stopped |
| Verification step understood | Yes, partly, no, or not assessed |
| Barrier reported | Free text without identifiers |
| Follow-up consent | Yes or no |
| Follow-up result at day 30 | Independently demonstrated, specific self-report, assisted use, no use, unreachable, or not yet due |

Keep the contact list separately and delete it when follow-up is complete. Set an agreed deletion date before collecting data. Do not publish individual records without explicit consent.

## 5. Measures to report honestly

- **Reach:** Number of consenting traders who saw a guide demonstration.
- **Independent task completion:** Number completing a task without assistance divided by those who attempted one. Report the denominator.
- **Verification understanding:** Number who can describe a relevant check before using the information.
- **Continued use:** Number demonstrating or giving a specific example of use at day 30, reported separately by evidence type and with unreachable participants counted transparently.
- **Inclusion:** Describe barriers by phone access, language, data affordability and literacy needs; avoid unsupported claims about improvement.

With a small pilot, report counts and observations. Do not claim statistically proven impact, income growth or reduced fraud without evidence.

## 6. Risks and responses

| Risk | Response |
| --- | --- |
| Stale or wrong price | Label source and date when verified; require a second confirmation; show no live price in the prototype. |
| Fraud or impersonation | Never verify a buyer from a phone number alone; confirm money in the trader's own payment account; direct suspected fraud to official providers. |
| Privacy | Ask consent; minimise records; keep contact details separate; do not store payment credentials or private messages. |
| Shared phone or low data | Allow guide-assisted practice, short sessions and a typed/tap fallback; measure whether independent access is feasible. |
| Yoruba speech errors | Confirm the transcript before acting; offer tap and text; review wording with native speakers. |
| Guide workload | Time sessions and ask guides what support and compensation would be sustainable before scaling. |

## 7. Two-minute pitch recording outline

Record a direct pitch following the current portal's restrictions; the portal says a video of at most two minutes and 500 MB, with no graphics or special editing. Use only consenting people on camera.

- **0:00–0:25 — Problem:** Market women can have access to mobile internet without using it for trade because text-heavy interfaces and unfamiliar online spaces feel irrelevant or risky.
- **0:25–0:50 — Solution:** AjoConnect trains trusted àjọ members as Digital Trade Guides. They teach practical voice or tap tasks in the trader's language.
- **0:50–1:25 — Demonstration:** Show a single task on a phone, preferably the price comparison. State that the displayed prices are fictional practice figures and explain the two-source verification step.
- **1:25–1:50 — Pilot evidence plan:** Explain the ladder: observe, complete a task independently, then check use at day 30. Mention real observations only if collected.
- **1:50–2:00 — Close:** Explain how the guide model could be repeated with another consenting àjọ group if the first pilot works.

The video should demonstrate the actual intervention and its current readiness. Do not imply that a completed pilot or live market feed exists.
